Amber & Ash

Bread comes out at seven. Most days, it doesn't last till noon.

Amber & Ash is a small wood-fired bakery and coffee counter — flour, water, salt, and a little patience, plus coffee roasted three streets over. No shortcuts, no mystery ingredients, just the four things done properly.

What we're about

We started Amber & Ash because we missed bread that tasted like something — crackling crust, an open crumb, the smell that pulls people in off the sidewalk. Everything is baked in a wood-fired oven we built ourselves, in small batches, most mornings before the neighborhood is fully awake. When the day's bake is gone, it's gone; we'd rather sell out than freeze dough to stretch the day.

Built in a garage, one bag of flour at a time.

In 2021, a baker and a coffee roaster with too many opinions about both decided to stop arguing about whose craft mattered more and open a counter that did both properly.

How it started

Mara had spent six years baking in restaurant kitchens and kept getting told her sourdough was "too rustic" for the plate. Devon had been roasting coffee in a converted shed and selling it out of the trunk of his car on Saturdays. They shared a landlord, complained to each other about the same things, and eventually just combined the ovens.

The wood-fired oven in the shop now was built by hand over four months, mostly by trial, error, and a mason friend who owed Devon a favor.

What we won't do

We won't bulk-ferment overnight in a walk-in to save labor, and we won't call something "artisan" because it has flour on it. If a loaf doesn't rise the way it should, we don't sell it — we eat our mistakes, literally.

We buy flour from a mill two hours away that still stone-grinds in small batches, and we tell you exactly which one, because it changes the bread and you should know why yours tastes different from anyone else's.

What we hold to

Small batches

We bake what we can sell fresh in a day. Nothing goes into a freezer to stretch further.

Named sources

Every flour, bean, and butter on our shelf has a farm or mill we can point to.

Honest hours

When we're out, we close the case. We'd rather disappoint you at 11 than sell you something stale.

Come find us.

We're on the corner, past the hardware store, with a hand-painted sign and usually a small line out front by 7:15.
